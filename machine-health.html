// 01 — MAINTENANCE WITH CONTEXT

See the change.
Plan the response.

A measurement becomes useful when it helps your team decide what to inspect, when to intervene, and which assets need attention first.

[ REACTIVE ]

Respond after failure.

Maintenance begins when equipment stops or a fault becomes visible. The interruption drives the work.

[ TIME-BASED ]

Follow the calendar.

Inspection and service follow a fixed schedule. Timing is set by an interval rather than measured condition.

[ CONDITION-BASED ]

Use measured behavior.

Trends and changes in machine condition help prioritize inspection and support better-timed maintenance.

// 02 — ONE MACHINE. MULTIPLE SIGNALS.

A fuller picture
of machine behavior.

Choose complementary measurements for the asset and the diagnostic question. Sensor count alone does not establish diagnostic capability.

[ 01 / VIBRATION ]

Understand mechanical response.

Capture motion, force transmission, and structural behavior. Evaluate bandwidth, sampling rate, resolution, and access to waveforms or frequency analysis.

[ 02 / ACOUSTIC ]

Listen for relevant changes.

Assess sound characteristics using the frequency response and sensor technology suited to the use case, including audible or ultrasonic measurement where appropriate.

[ 03 / TEMPERATURE ]

Add thermal context.

Track heat generation and dissipation with measurement range, accuracy, repeatability, and physical placement matched to the asset.

[ 04 / EDGE INTELLIGENCE ]

Turn changes into evidence.

Relate signals to operating behavior through baselines, trends, and anomaly analysis. Validate thresholds and fault insights before relying on them in maintenance decisions.

// 04 — ENGINEER THE WHOLE DEPLOYMENT

More than
a sensor count.

Five dimensions guide a practical evaluation: measurement, intelligence, integration, robustness, and lifecycle economics.

01 Measurement quality

Frequency range, sampling rate, dynamic range, noise floor, resolution, and RMS / waveform / FFT availability. Match acoustic response and temperature accuracy to the installation and diagnostic task.

02 Validated intelligence

Evaluate baseline learning, anomaly detection, fault classification, and threshold selection against evidence from representative operating conditions.

03 Data access and integration

Specify raw measurements versus processed indicators, retention, buffering, APIs, wired or wireless connectivity, resilience, and cybersecurity requirements.

04 Installation robustness

Check enclosure rating, temperature limits, vibration and shock, humidity, oil or chemical exposure, power, wireless conditions, and mounting.

05 Lifecycle economics

Assess the full installed cost: sensors, gateways, software, installation, commissioning, maintenance, and eventual replacement.
